Conditionality kills 2AR strategy
Solt 2021 · Wake LD · p.412

Conditional advocacy in Lincoln-Douglas creates a structural inequity that runs deeper than the time-tradeoff arguments most negatives are prepared to answer. The 2AR is forced to invest offense across every shell the negative may or may not extend, producing a pre-allocation tax that scales with conditionality and that no 1AR pre-empt can recover. The result is not merely a strategic disadvantage but a procedural one: the affirmative cannot run a coherent strategy when the negative's ground is contingent on the very speech that was supposed to resolve it.

Critics counter that conditional advocacy improves topic exploration and that the 2AR may simply prioritize, but this assumes the affirmative knows in advance which positions the negative will collapse on. In practice, late-breaking kicks render 1AR investments worthless and 2AR concessions strategically lethal.

The fairness violation, then, is not the speed of the round but the architecture of the round. Conditionality changes what arguments mean by making their persistence conditional on a future negative speech the affirmative cannot influence. That is a procedural violation distinct from the educational ones, and it grounds the strongest version of the conditionality-bad shell.
